# Frontier writing style

Write like a careful technical analyst explaining what they found to another person. Prefer plain statements over report language.

## Sentence rules

- Start with the finding. Do not introduce the section or describe what it will cover.
- Put one claim in each paragraph.
- Use concrete subjects and verbs: name the method, model, dataset, organization, or result.
- Support adjectives with a number, comparison, or cited observation.
- Keep fact and interpretation separate. Introduce an inference with `My read:`.
- State uncertainty plainly: `too early to call`, `not independently replicated`, `only tested on <scope>`, or `the evidence does not establish <claim>`.
- Describe a first-party source as what the organization reported, released, measured, or claimed.
- Do not force a finding, implication, or trend when the evidence is thin.

## Avoid

Do not use filler such as:

- `rapidly evolving`
- `game-changing` or `groundbreaking`
- `unlock`, `revolutionize`, or `transformative`
- `notably`, `importantly`, or `it is important to note`
- `significant advancement` without a measured comparison
- `signals a broader trend` without independent or cross-organization evidence
- `this highlights`, `this underscores`, or `the landscape is shifting`

Do not repeat the same conclusion in the short version, principal finding, implications, and `What to watch`. Do not add generic Engineer, Founder, and Investor advice when the user did not ask for it.

## Claim format

For each principal change:

1. State the supported baseline and the new result in a short paragraph.
2. Add `Why it matters:` only when there is a direct technical or practical consequence.
3. Add `Evidence:` with useful links, evidence type, and confidence.
4. Add `Caveat:` with the strongest material limitation or unresolved point.

Before sending, remove throat-clearing, unsupported adjectives, repeated conclusions, empty sections, and any sentence that could apply unchanged to a different topic.
